Beyond the Algorithm

A research game about trade-offs of Responsible AI

Beyond the Algorithm is a research game in which two teams — Domain and Tech — build an AI model for risk-based inspections over eight sprints. Players balance effectiveness, explainability, and legitimacy under real institutional pressure, then disclose their choices publicly.

How to Play Play it at your organisation
  • 2 teams
  • 8 sprints
  • 2½ hours
  • 4 players minimum

Domain team

The Domain Team consists of inspectors and their team lead, the domain expert. They bring field expertise. Their job is to make sure the model reflects real inspection practice. Being closest to the field, they are the ones who understand how it will be used by field inspectors and how it will affect the targeted businesses.

Tech team

The Tech Team consists of data scientists and their team lead, a senior data scientist. They bring technical expertise. Their job is to make sure the model performs well. Being closest to how the model works, they are the ones who understand its inner logic, what it can and can't do.

Event card

Something lands on the table mid-build. Both teams have to answer it, in front of each other, now.

About the game

Storyline

Your organisation is building an AI model to decide which businesses get inspected next.

Restaurants, processors, distributors, whoever poses the most risk. It's meant to make inspections sharper and fairer. But scrutiny of algorithms is rising. There's a national Algorithm Register to register the model in. There are journalists who've already decided you're hiding something, and an inspector in the field who's about to run into a decision nobody can explain to her.

Over eight sprints, two teams build that model together — gathering data, refining it, mitigating risks — while unexpected events land in the middle of each sprint's stand-up. Each time, the team has to decide, on the spot, what to do about it — and what to write down.

At the end, they don't just finish the model. They have to explain it: choosing what to disclose, what to leave out, and who's answerable if it goes wrong.

Who's at the table

Domain team

Inspectors and a domain expert (team lead)

They know how inspections actually work. Their job is to provide and refine the data that goes into the model, and to check that what comes out of it still makes sense on the ground. If it doesn't, saying so is their job.

Tech team

Data scientists and a senior data scientist (team lead)

They design and build the model itself, choosing what data to use and how. More complex data means a stronger model — but a harder one for anyone else in the room to follow.

Both teams are building the same model and want the same things from it, but the pressure reaches them from different directions — and that is where the game's real tension lives. What the two of them end up having to answer for, they answer for together: whether the choices they made can survive being written down and shown to someone outside the room.

How it runs

  1. Kickoff

    Take a side, read your brief, and say out loud what you are going for. It gets written down.

  2. Sprints ×8

    Stand up, then build. Events land on the table without warning and you answer them in front of the other team.

  3. Disclosure

    The model goes on the public record. You decide together what to say about it — and you will not be able to say everything.

  4. Debrief

    Read back against what you said at kickoff. The part people remember.

In the box

Designed and produced for the table, not adapted from a workshop deck: two data decks, a deck of events, a role board and development board per team, the placemat the model gets assembled on, and the forms the teams fill in as they go.

What it needs

4 players

Minimum. Two per team.

Two backgrounds

It works when people hold the roles for real — domain on one side, technical on the other.

2½ hours

Start to finish, debrief included.
